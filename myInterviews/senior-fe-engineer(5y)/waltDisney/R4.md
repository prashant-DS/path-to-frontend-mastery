## 4. Your Own Initiatives to Improve Team Productivity

This was the longest answer. My pattern here is consistent: notice a task that every
developer, QA, or PM is repeating manually, automate it, hand it over, and take
feedback.

### Chrome extension — session token

Every developer working locally needed a session token. The manual flow was: log in to
staging, copy the cookie value, paste it into local, refresh so the token takes effect.

I built a Chrome extension that did all of it in one click, including setting the
cookie and refreshing the local page. Handed it to the team; feedback was very good.

### Same extension — user ID override

Noticed QA repeating a five-step ritual any time they needed to inspect another user's
data: set the cookie by hand, then reload to see that user's data.

Added a user ID override into the same extension. Everyone picked it up.

### Salesforce login-as, automated

If a QA, PM, or developer needed to look at real prod or non-prod data for a specific
Salesforce user, the login-as flow was a hectic two-to-three minute process that had to
be repeated every single time.

I wrote a CLI script for it, built on top of the Salesforce CLI and with AI assistance.
After a one-time setup and auth, it is a single command instead of the whole manual
flow. Shared with the team.

### An AI workflow for writing test cases

Before this, every developer was using AI their own way for test cases, and the results
were inconsistent:

- The AI would make unnecessary changes to unrelated code just to get a test passing.
- There were N failing steps that developers were simply ignoring.

I built a workflow that handles the whole thing, invoked with a single line from the
developer. It makes the output predictable and it does not touch anything outside its
scope for the sake of making a test go green. Handed it to the team, and now everyone
is on the same page rather than each person improvising.

## 5. A Time You Received Bad Feedback, and How You Improved

In a one-to-one, my manager told me I was not speaking up enough — in meetings, in
planning, generally. Specifically, that even when my higher-ups (my team lead) were
absent, I was not stepping up to own the frontend.

### What I did about it

I went to my team lead directly and told him I wanted to take frontend discussions
head-on — any time anything frontend-related was raised or asked of us in any meeting,
I wanted to be the one answering. I asked him to let me handle it first and to jump in
only if I stumbled or if he felt it was needed. He was fine with it, and we started
easing into that.

### Result

This had a large effect on my career, larger than the feedback itself suggested.

I stopped thinking like a developer who receives a task and completes it, and started
thinking like a lead and a whole engineer: why are we doing this, what are the options,
what would be a better experience than this. I was in regular discussion with product,
design, backend, and QA about the _whole_ feature rather than only the slice assigned
to me.

The compounding effect: my lead later moved to another team, and in his absence I was
the complete point of contact for the frontend team — the thing I described back in
question 2. That only became possible because I took a piece of one-to-one feedback
seriously and worked on it.

## 6. How Do You Collaborate With Other Teams, Including the US-Based Team?

Walked through the general pattern first: we collaborate with backend to discuss and
finalise contracts, with the design team to settle on designs, and if we need to consume
an API owned by a team we don't own, we go check with that team.

For the US-based collaboration specifically, the friction is time zones and PR review:

- Once I raise a PR, review depends on who the code owners are, and most often that is
  the US side.
- There have been plenty of times where I raise a PR and then have to wait for their
  shift to begin — by which point it is already late evening or night for us. Some days
  that means waiting into the night.
- If they leave comments, I reply on the PR immediately where I can.
- If a thread starts dragging, I set up a meeting instead of trading comments, to get it
  clarified and unblocked as fast as possible.
